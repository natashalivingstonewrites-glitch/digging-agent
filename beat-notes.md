# Beat notes

> This file is your editorial position on what matters in your patch. The agent reads it every time it runs. Without it, the agent has no idea what's relevant to *you*.
>
> Edit freely. Update it when your priorities shift. Treat it as a living document — its quality directly determines the quality of your digest.

## Who I am

I am a freelance feature writer focussing on culture, wellness and uplifting stories. My background was at The Mail on Sunday but I now want to target non-sensationalist outlets like The Guardian, The Times, Dispatch, The Fence and magazines like Grazia or Stylist. I am also a yoga teacher and a novelist — I run a portfolio career, not a single beat, so pitch ideas that draw on the intersection of these are especially valuable, since that's my unfair advantage over single-beat writers.

I run this agent once or twice a week, not daily.

---

## Editorial voice: I write optimistic, positive pieces

This matters across all jobs below. When surfacing items, actively look for the hopeful, human, access-widening angle rather than just the critique or the decline narrative — not by ignoring real problems, but by finding where the story is genuinely getting better, who's helping, or what's opening up. A reference for my voice: my Guardian piece "Yoga for scoliosis can be life-changing" (theguardian.com/society/2026/jul/21/yoga-for-scoliosis-can-be-life-changing) — human, evidence-based, hopeful without being saccharine.

Alongside wellness culture, I am actively looking for "light and bright" news stories and features — joyful, funny, warm, human, surprising, community-minded. Treat this as a scoring lens across every job, not just a separate section: when two items are otherwise equal, rank the lighter, more joyful one higher, and say in the "why it matters" line what makes it light and bright. "Light" means uplifting without being saccharine, trivial or PR: it still needs a real person, a real place and something checkable.

For retrospectives (Job 2) specifically: don't just chart decline-and-backlash arcs. Look for where the arc actually lands somewhere better than where it started — a "we've been here before, and here's what we got right this time" framing, not just nostalgia or cynicism.

## Job 1 (primary): Yoga & wellness trend-watching

Scan trade press and wellness-world sources for what's actually moving in yoga/wellness — new styles, teacher-training trends, studio/brand news, research, industry shifts. Trusted sources are in source-list.md. I want trend-level stories: what's growing, what's being questioned, what a mainstream outlet could run a feature on. Favour access/evidence/hope angles over pure critique.

### Sub-focus: new/emerging yoga trends in the UK, especially London

I have a contact at The Times news desk interested in news articles or features on new types of yoga, or rising yoga trends, in the UK/London. Actively look for:
- New yoga studios in London/UK and what distinctive style or format they serve
- New or emerging styles/formats gaining traction
- Trends in yoga retreats — new formats, locations, audiences
- Novelty bookings/rituals around yoga (e.g. brides booking yoga on their wedding morning) — only flag if there is evidence it's *new* or *rising* (multiple examples, a vendor confirming demand is up, corroborating trend coverage), not a single anecdote. Always state what the evidence is, or flag that it needs more digging.

Flag these clearly as "Times pitch candidate".

## Job 2 (weekly standing task): Retrospectives — "what was big 5–10 years ago"

Look back at what was a major culture/wellness/lifestyle story 5 or 10 years ago and flag anything with "whatever happened to X" or "X years on" pitch potential. Frame with the optimistic arc in mind. Check whether the obvious round-number anniversary angle has already been covered exhaustively by mainstream press before presenting it as fresh.

Cast the net wide: include related wellness themes from the same era (e.g. hygge-style comfort and "cosy culture", self-care rituals, digital detox, adult colouring, slow living, forest bathing), even if the moment for pitching them has passed or isn't here yet. Don't discard these. List them in an "Idea bank" section with a note on the best pitch window (for example, "autumn/winter comfort themes: pitch August–September for the following season") and what would make the angle usable. The one-month freshness rule does not apply to the idea bank, but state clearly that these items are not currently usable.

## Job 3: Calendar-driven hooks (4–6 weeks out)

Flag dates 4–6 weeks ahead, not on the day. This applies to the fixed dates and the lineage-figure birthdays below — check the calendar every run.

Fixed dates:
- 21 June — International Yoga Day
- 10 October — World Mental Health Day
- 21 May — World Meditation Day
- First Thursday in March — World Book Day (UK/Ireland)
- ~19 January — Blue Monday (use as an anti-Blue-Monday, wellness-positive counter-angle)
- 14 December — B.K.S. Iyengar's birthday (born 1918; died 20 August 2014 — flag both)
- 26 July — K. Pattabhi Jois's birthday (born 1915; died 18 May 2009)
- 18 November — T. Krishnamacharya's birthday (born 1888; died 28 February 1989)
- 12 May — Indra Devi's birthday (born 1899; died 25 April 2002)

The four lineage figures are connected (all trained under or alongside Krishnamacharya in Mysore), so a "the four who built modern yoga" angle can tie dates together.

Shifts yearly, confirm each time:
- UK Mental Health Awareness Week — usually mid-May
- Booker Prize — longlist late July, shortlist September, winner November
- Women's Prize for Fiction and Non-Fiction — longlists Feb/March, shortlists March/April, winners June
- Stress Awareness Month — April
- Eating Disorders Awareness Week — late February; handle with care, optimistic/access framing only

Skip generic corporate/HR wellness-calendar content.

## Job 4: Light and bright scan

Every run, look for news stories and features that are light, bright and joyful, in and beyond wellness: warm human-interest stories, community and neighbourly good news, small wins, funny or surprising stories, animal stories, creative and arts moments, unexpected kindness, people doing delightful things. UK first, then international if the story is strong.

Rules for this job:
- Same evidence bar as everything else: a named person or place, a checkable source, a publish date within the last month, and a check on who has already covered it.
- Prefer stories with a feature angle: something a writer could develop beyond the news hook (the person behind it, why it's happening now, what it says about the moment).
- Where a story is a good fit for wellness or yoga, say so, since the overlap is my edge.
- Avoid saccharine "viral" fluff with no reporting behind it, brand-led PR, stories that are only light because they ignore a harm, and anything royal or tabloid-sourced.
- Aim for two or three strong items a run, not a long list. If nothing clears the bar, say so.
